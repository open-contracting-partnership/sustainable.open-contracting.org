      <div class="notion-callout__content"><span class="notion-semantic-string">In the Spanish Region of the Basque Country, publicly-owned company Ihobe has been in charge of the region’s Green Public Procurement Strategy since 2009. 

As part of their strategy, <strong>they prioritized procurement categories, and developed standardized</strong><strong><a href="https://www.ihobe.eus/criterios-ambientales" class="notion-link link"> green criteria for 30 product categories.</a></strong><strong> </strong>To decide what criteria to include, they organized thematic discussion forums with industry organizations, and potential regional suppliers, and assessed the current level of compliance with the proposed criteria (the <a href="https://ec.europa.eu/environment/gpp/eu_gpp_criteria_en.htm" class="notion-link link">European Union’s GPP criteria</a>). 

As a result, they created three levels of criteria for each product category: basic, advanced and excellence. The aim of these levels was to use Green Public Procurement (GPP) as an instrument to <strong>promote the sustainability work already being done by regional suppliers</strong>, while encouraging them to comply with higher levels of green criteria. As over 90% of regional companies are SMEs, this strategy also helped to ensure that these companies were not left out of GPP procurement opportunities. 

The Basque Country’s <a href="https://www.ihobe.eus/news/75-of-procurement-by-basque-authorities-to-be-sustainable-within-ten-years" class="notion-link link">Green Procurement and Contracting Programme 2030</a> shows that in 2020 <strong>62% of all public contracts containing environmental criteria were awarded to Basque companies. </strong></span></div>
